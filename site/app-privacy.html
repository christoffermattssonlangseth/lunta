In short

Lunta collects nothing about you. There are no accounts, no analytics, no ads and no trackers. Your library, highlights and notes are yours and stay in your own storage.

Where your library is kept

  • On iPhone, iPad and Mac, in your own iCloud Drive. Apple stores it under your Apple Account; the developer of Lunta has no access to it.
  • On Android, Windows and Linux (and on a Mac that bridges to them), in a “Lunta” folder in your own Google Drive, if you choose to connect it. Lunta can only see the files it creates there (and a folder you choose for it in Google’s file picker), not the rest of your Drive. It uses that access only to keep the “Lunta” folder in sync, and the sign-in tokens stay on your device.

What Lunta sends over the internet

To find papers and their details, Lunta looks them up in public scholarly services, sending only what it is looking up (a DOI, a title, search words, the topics you follow in New for You): OpenAlex, Crossref, Europe PMC and PubMed Central, PubMed, arXiv, bioRxiv/medRxiv, eLife, and doi.org. To download open-access PDFs and figures it contacts the publisher or archive that hosts them. These requests carry no personal information, but like any web request they reveal your IP address to that service.

  • Ask (the assistant) runs on your device: with Apple Intelligence, or with a free open model that Lunta downloads once (from Hugging Face) and then runs offline. Your questions and papers are not sent to any server.
  • Natural voices for listening are downloaded once and then run on your device. On Windows, Linux and Android the voice (and on Windows and Linux its speech engine) comes from the sherpa-onnx project's releases on GitHub; that download is the only request it makes, and nothing you listen to leaves the device.
  • Updates (Mac, Windows, Linux and Android, as downloaded from GitHub): once a day, unless you turn it off in Settings, and when you choose Check for Updates, Lunta asks GitHub, where it is published, for the latest version, and downloads it from GitHub if you install it. Only the app's version goes with the request. The iPhone and iPad app is updated by the App Store.
  • The browser extension and the Word and Google Docs add-ins talk only to the Lunta app on your own computer, and, when you cite in Google Docs with the extension, to Google Docs (below).

Citing in Google Docs with the browser extension

When you connect your Google account in the extension's panel in Google Docs, the extension gets Google's permission to see and edit your Google Docs documents (https://www.googleapis.com/auth/documents). It uses it only for the document you are citing in, and only when you click Insert Citation, Insert Bibliography or Update All, or change the citation style: it reads the document to find its citations and writes the formatted citations, the bibliography and the details of the cited papers (kept in the document, so co-authors can update it) back into that document. The document's content goes only between your browser and Google, and the cited papers' ids and details to the Lunta app on your own computer to be formatted. Nothing is sent to us or anyone else, stored outside the document, used for advertising, or read by people. The access token stays in your browser until it quits; you can remove the permission any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

In Safari on the Mac, the Lunta app connects to Google instead of the extension (Safari doesn't let extensions sign in to Google), with the same permission used the same way: the extension passes your clicks to the Lunta app on your Mac, which reads and writes the document you are citing in. The document's content then goes only between Google and the Lunta app on your Mac. The app keeps its sign-in in your Mac's Keychain until you disconnect or remove the permission.

Lunta's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

On your device

Lunta stores its settings, a search index and caches on your device. Notifications (the morning New for You notice) are scheduled on the device. Deleting the app removes them; your library stays in your iCloud or Google Drive until you delete it there.

Children

Lunta is a research tool and is not directed at children.

Changes and contact

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with its date. Questions: GitHub Issues.
